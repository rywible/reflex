# Retain Candidate Fates and Allocation attribution

The Experience Ledger retains a compact Candidate Fate for every generated Candidate in each completed selection transaction and records which deterministic Allocation Queue selected work for Verification. Candidate Fates distinguish exact novelty-filter reasons, budget-deferred, Verified, admitted, and strict-improvement outcomes while queue attribution distinguishes protected exploration, learned ranking, and Bootstrap ranking. The durable epoch is a monotonically increasing selection-transaction identity, restored from retained Experience independently of the Pareto observer sequence. A Verified fate retains its Verification batch's total accounted CPU and batch size without pretending that batched work has a measured per-Candidate cost. All Verified fates in an epoch must agree on that attribution and their retained count must equal the declared batch size. These observations remain immutable; versioned derivation produces Training Targets later. Fatal Domain or Verification errors and work abandoned before selection do not publish partial Experience.

This makes the policy that actually ran attributable and replayable without exposing experimental controls through the public Improvement Session or Domain Definition interfaces. Reordering the historically selected, verifier-labeled subset remains an off-policy diagnostic, not causal evidence for a deployed policy. Causal claims require an equal-budget online comparison such as the Reference Domain treatment. The Runtime Controller remains the sole Allocation authority, and the Verification Kernel remains the sole correctness authority.

## Considered options

Inferring fates from Verified Experience loses Candidates filtered or deferred before Verification. Reconstructing queue attribution from final model scores cannot reproduce the policy that actually ran, especially after Model or Knowledge Revision changes. Keeping traces only in process telemetry would be cheaper, but would make retained-state ablations and Operational Promotion evidence irreproducible after restart.

The Reference Domain supplies multiple deliberately refutable probe Operators so protected claim exploration cannot remove the sole negative example from the ordinary Allocation Queue. This is part of that Domain's production role: it makes causal learned-allocation behavior identifiable through the public Improvement Session without changing the frozen Lean experiment's Operators.

Candidate Fate and queue attribution change restart-complete Experience semantics. Existing bundles cannot supply truthful attribution retroactively, so Runtime revision 6 deliberately rejects revision-5 bundles rather than fabricating evidence. This decision supersedes the byte-compatibility clause of ADR 0046 for this pre-1.0 semantic migration; the public library interface remains unchanged.
